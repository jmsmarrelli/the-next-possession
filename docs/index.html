Seven questions between San Antonio and a title.

Python and R projects built around a defending Western Conference champion, a generational two-way centerpiece, and an unfinished Finals story.

Portfolio thesis

San Antonio enters 2026–27 as a defending Western Conference champion with a real two-way title profile. These seven projects turn that context into a coherent decision system: where the schedule applies pressure, which contenders define the bar, which rotation combinations deserve deeper study, how much outcomes move with Wembanyama’s availability, why the Finals slipped, where roster archetypes overlap, and what a transparent preseason simulation implies.

Headline outputs

  • Schedule: 80 assigned games, 13 back-to-backs, about 53,962 travel miles.
  • Prior-year level: No. 2 adjusted margin, +8.24 per 100.
  • Finals pressure point: 18.8-point average peak-to-final swing.
  • Simulation baseline: 54.1 wins in assigned games; No. 2 among modeled title contenders.

Each project has paired Python and R entry points, an analysis-ready CSV, methods and limitations, and a rendered visual. The build is deterministic at seed 210.

Why this season

The 2025–26 Spurs went 62–20, beat Oklahoma City in seven games to win the West, then lost a closer-than-4–1 Finals to New York. Victor Wembanyama earned First-Team All-NBA and Defensive Player of the Year honors; Keldon Johnson won Sixth Man of the Year. The 2026–27 schedule opens at home against the Thunder and includes a Christmas Finals rematch at Madison Square Garden plus games in Paris and Manchester.

That is the portfolio’s through-line: turn last year’s breakthrough and last possession failures into this year’s decision questions.
